---
title: 'Are Humans Truly Altruistic?'
tags: 'Life, Journal, Social dynamics, Psychology, Philosophy'
date: '2025-09-12'
--------------------
Most of the time, I’ve noticed that almost everyone I meet is self-obsessed — always talking about themselves. Even in conversations, it happens: someone says something, and we immediately bring in our own story, opinion, or experience. I do it too. And then I wonder — are we actually listening, or are we just waiting for our turn to talk?


But then again, if a conversation was just one person talking and the other only listening, it would feel boring. A friend once told me that. While we were discussing this, the same friend brought up a concept called _altruism_ — putting others’ well-being above your own immediate interests.


And then my friend said: _“Honestly, I don’t think pure altruism really exists. Almost everything we do has some selfish core. Even when we gift someone something, it’s partly because their happiness makes us happy.”_


That made me think. Maybe humans really are built selfish, or at least self-focused. Still, I often notice how people — including me — like to present certain actions as if they’re selfless. Not everything is disguised that way, but when it happens, I can’t help wondering why. When I catch myself being self-centered, I sometimes try to shift and act more “altruistic.” But then I question — is there even a reason to do that, if deep down, everyone’s the same?


Maybe the real answer is simpler: we just express ourselves in ways that make us feel good. Maybe that’s what being human really is.
