title: 'Weight of existing '
tags: 'Journal, Social dynamics, Daily life, Psychology, Philosophy'
date: '2025-08-28'
--------------------
No matter how careful we are, just by living in society we inevitably affect others, sometimes negatively. That’s the raw truth. People like to pretend they can be “neutral,” but in reality, being alive already consumes space, attention, and resources.


One of the things I hate most is the thought of being a burden, or causing harm to people—even unintentionally. Even if we don’t actively help, just existing means we will, at some point, inconvenience or weigh on others. Maybe that’s one of the unavoidable aspects of being human.


The only way I see to balance that is by leaning the other way: doing good, as much as possible, as often as possible. Even the smallest acts—helping, sharing, showing kindness—tip the scale in the right direction.


As a bachelor sharing a room with roommates, I see this play out in daily life. There are always tiny clashes—maybe someone didn’t do the dishes, maybe something small was left undone. Sometimes you’re the one annoyed, sometimes it’s the other person. The little things matter more than we admit, because they shape how people feel about living with you.


In the end, adjustments are everything. Each of us lives in our own world, shaped by our own thoughts, routines, and habits. That naturally makes us selfish at times—we don’t, or maybe can’t, always think about the other person’s world. But when these different worlds collide in the same room or apartment, the only way forward is through adjustment. It’s the bridge that lets people with different rhythms coexist without breaking the connection.
