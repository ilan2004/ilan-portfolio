title: 'Would you do it even if you had a million every month ? '
tags: 'Life, Tinkering, Engineering, Money'
date: '2025-08-27'
--------------------
Last week, some of us friends were just hanging out and talking. That’s when this question popped up: what would you do if money was no longer a worry? Would you still be doing engineering?


It’s a famous, almost cliché question. But the reason it sticks around is because it’s an important one.


I’ve seen many YouTube videos on this theme. One that often shows up in my feed is Robert Waldinger’s [TED Talk on the Harvard Study of Adult Development](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KkKuTCFvzI) — one of the longest-running studies on happiness. They followed people for decades, from Harvard students to middle-class families, to understand what really makes a good life. The answer wasn’t money or status, but something deeper.


That got me thinking about engineering.


Engineering is the application of science and mathematics to design, build, and optimize systems, machines, structures, or processes that solve practical problems.


But for different people, “engineering” means different things:

- For some, it’s a career path → stability, salary, social standing.
- For others, it’s a craft → building, tinkering, innovating.
- For a few, it’s a mission → shaping the future, solving humanity’s biggest challenges.

Take Elon Musk, for example. He already has more money than he could possibly need, yet he’s still building and breaking things. And it’s not just him — many billionaires and even trillionaires keep pushing, creating, and experimenting. Why?


Whenever I’ve built or broken something myself — whether it was a game mod, a piece of software, or just some random idea that came to me — I never did it because of money. In fact, most of those things never made me a single rupee.


Was it for people? For social validation? Maybe a little. But honestly, most of the time it wasn’t even that — because not everything I tinkered with was ever shown to people, or even seen by them.


And that’s the thing about tinkering. It’s not always about outcomes or recognition. It’s that little voice of “what if?” — the same question babies ask when they touch, pull, and experiment with the world. That “what if” instinct is in our DNA. It’s the reason humans have reached this point in evolution. Even the tiniest what if can grow into something fascinating, useful, or just plain cool. And most of the time, the meaning isn’t there at the start — the meaning shows up only after it’s done.


For me, it’s that inner pull — curiosity, the challenge of testing myself, proving to myself that I can do something, and then feeling that spark of satisfaction when I actually pull it off.


And that leads me back to the question.


So, would I still do it if I had a million every month?


Yes.


 I’d still be building, breaking, tinkering.
