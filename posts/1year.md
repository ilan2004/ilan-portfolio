Exactly a year ago, I started this blog. Back then, I wasn’t sure where it would go — whether it would just be another abandoned side project, or something that would stick. A year later, it has become a space I keep returning to.

For me, writing here isn’t about being perfect or profound. It’s about documenting — the raw, the real, the unfinished thoughts. These posts are little anchors of my mind at different times. Reading them back is like flipping through old photographs — except instead of faces and places, I’m revisiting my mindset, my worries, my optimism, and my experiments.

Some entries make me cringe, some surprise me, and some I’m still proud of. But together, they form a timeline of growth.

> “Writing is thinking. To write well is to think clearly.” — David McCullough

Here’s what writing has taught me over the past year:

* Clarity doesn’t come before writing, it comes during writing.
* Publishing online forces me to be a little sharper and braver.
* Even the smallest ideas feel bigger once they’re written down.
* A blog post is like planting a seed — sometimes it grows into something I never expected.
* Writing is a mirror — it shows me who I am becoming.

![](/images/journal-desk.jpg)

### Why I Keep Writing

* To slow down my thoughts and organize them
* To leave behind a trail of my journey
* To collect and revisit good ideas
* To turn learning into something permanent
* To improve my ability to explain
* To share with others who might resonate
* To increase my “surface area of luck” — letting ideas and people find me
* To build consistency and discipline
* To discover my own writing voice
* To connect dots across projects, books, and experiences
