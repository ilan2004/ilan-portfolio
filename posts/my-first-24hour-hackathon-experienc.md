Yesterday, I completed my first ever 24-hour hackathon. Technically, it wasn’t my first attempt — the first one I went for was Ethereum-based, but since my team wasn’t familiar with the tech, we ended up dropping out midway. This time, though, we saw it through.


Our idea was to build an app that combined MBTI personality types with focus tracking — basically, personalising app features based on the personality obtained from the MBTI test. It was something we believed in and put our hearts into.


And let me tell you — the experience was intense. Competing with everything you’ve got, fighting for something by actually building it… that’s such a cool feeling. It reminded me why I love engineering so much. Creating something you believe in, no matter how small, is refreshing in a way that’s hard to explain.


Of course, it wasn’t all smooth.

- We arrived late because multiple Uber drivers cancelled on us, and the one we finally got was painfully slow.
- We started last.
- At one point, after finishing the entire backend, everything got wiped out due to a GitHub mistake.
- During judging, our Groq API failed — all because I had left my VPN on.

Despite all this, we pushed through. Even the tiredness from staying up for 24 hours straight couldn’t stop us from laughing at half the chaos we ran into.


The hackathon itself was well-organised in terms of food and hospitality, but the judging was… let’s just say questionable. It was handled by the club members themselves, and there was no clear sense of criteria. That was the only sour note in an otherwise great experience.


But in the end, I walked away with something much bigger: the rush of building, the thrill of competition, and the reminder of how much I love this whole process.


Exhausting? Absolutely.


Unpredictable? Definitely.


Worth it? Without a doubt.
