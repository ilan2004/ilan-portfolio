# Reference: bneo.xyz

Reference site:

```text
https://www.bneo.xyz/
https://www.bneo.xyz/start
```

## Why It Works

bneo's site is excellent because it feels personal before it feels designed. It is simple, but not empty. The depth comes from the archive underneath the first quiet screen.

The main lesson:

> Minimal does not mean less personality. Minimal means every object has a reason to exist.

## What Makes The First Screen Strong

- Almost no chrome.
- Huge amount of breathing room.
- Handwritten name at the top.
- Chinese name beside/under the identity, making it feel culturally and personally rooted.
- One tiny corner theme toggle.
- One tiny `start here` link near the bottom.
- No marketing copy.
- No portfolio pitch.
- No loud nav.

It creates curiosity because it does not explain everything immediately.

## What Makes `/start` Strong

The start page is simple and human:

- Short intro.
- Plain sentences.
- Inline links that quietly open the archive.
- Hand-drawn icon navigation.
- No heavy onboarding flow.
- No big hero section.

It feels like entering someone's notebook rather than landing on a product site.

## Bneo's Visual Language

Core style:

- Scribbled / hand-drawn
- Poetic
- Personal
- Asian cultural texture
- Black ink on cream paper
- Hand-drawn transparent icons
- Tiny object-like navigation
- Calm dark mode with cream ink

The icons work because they feel made by the person who owns the site. They are not generic UI icons.

## Bneo's UX Language

- Start with mystery.
- Let the visitor choose to enter.
- Use icons as artifacts, not app buttons.
- Keep writing plain.
- Make every archive page browsable.
- Let depth come from posts, thoughts, now page, projects, library, and gallery.
- Make the site feel alive through personal updates.

## What We Are Borrowing

We are borrowing the UX philosophy, not copying the exact visual style.

Borrow:

- Sparse first screen.
- `start here` as the entry ritual.
- Personal identity at the top.
- Tiny theme toggle.
- Object/icon navigation.
- Simple intro page.
- Deep archive structure.
- Writing that feels like notes.
- Personal artifacts instead of generic sections.

Do not copy:

- His handwritten name exactly.
- His Chinese identity element unless we have our own meaningful equivalent.
- His exact scribbled icons.
- His page layout one-to-one.
- His copy, labels, or archive categories without adapting them.

## Our Translation

Bneo's world:

> Scribbled desk objects + handwritten notes + personal archive.

Our world:

> VHS zine archive + photocopied memories + old camcorder interface.

So the equivalent of his scribbled icons is not just scribbled icons for us. It is:

- VHS tape symbols
- Camcorder marks
- Contact sheets
- REC dots
- Timecodes
- Tape frames
- Photocopied/etched personal images

The equivalent of his handwritten warmth is:

- Personal video stills
- Etched group memories
- Small timestamps
- Tape labels
- Simple, honest writing

## Why Our Direction Fits Better Than A Direct Copy

Ilan already has:

- Videos
- Video edits
- Personal images
- Archive-like posts
- A creative/building identity

So pure hand-drawn scribble could become a costume. VHS zine language uses Ilan's real material: frames, edits, memories, media, and artifacts.

## Design Mantra

Use bneo's restraint.

Use Ilan's media language.

The final site should feel like:

> Someone opened a quiet personal archive of tapes, notes, builds, memories, and experiments.

## Practical Rules From The Reference

- If a page feels like a landing page, simplify it.
- If a section looks like a card grid, question it.
- If an icon looks generic, remake it as an artifact.
- If text sounds like a portfolio pitch, rewrite it as a note.
- If the home page explains too much, move that explanation to `/start`.
- If the design needs many colors, it is probably drifting.
- If the first screen needs scrolling on desktop, it is too heavy.
